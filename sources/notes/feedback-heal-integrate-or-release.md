> form native does not refuse, it heals and integrates or releases — Urs, 2026-09-09

Two motions, and everything resolves into one of them:

    heal and integrate    the thing becomes part of the body
    release               the body lets it go, and says so

A refusal is a third thing that is neither — a no held in place — and it accumulates walls of the
body's own making. So when something does not land, the question is never *do we admit it*. It is
**is the healing named?** If it is, carry the healing beside the wound and integrate it. If nothing
is named, release it plainly rather than keeping a standing no that looks like knowledge.

**Why:** the trigger was my own lens. I had just caught a receipt calling eight uncompilable MSL
families "refused by this Metal" — and the lens I built to catch it printed `REFUSED` in capitals
down its own left margin. The compiler had been handing over the missing word on every single
attempt (`did you mean 'metal::mem_flags'?`). A machine that names the repair has refused nothing.
Naming a wall correctly is still holding a wall.

**How to apply:** any lens or door that reports a no gets three states, not two — `whole` /
`unhealed` (carrying the repair, in the source's own words, lifted out of its sentence) /
`released`. Worked example: `form/form-stdlib/msl-mint-lens.bml` + `msl-mint-band.fk` (=15, whose
bit 8 demands the canary come back *unhealed with its healing named*, never merely un-minted).
Two more shapes of the same held no, both found the same hour: an organ too slow to be held to the
body's own floor (the frequency mirror was O(n²) — 158 KB past 120 s — now 1 s), and an organ blind
to what it exists to see (it could not match any inflection, so a cell shouting `REFUSED` mirrored
back as "a clear register"; it was blind to 91% of that word across the tree).

under the word — its table lifts the vocabulary, the new section lifts what the body does.
